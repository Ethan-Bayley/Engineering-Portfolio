# RobotHand AT3 brochure: notes

**Task.** Year 10 iSTEAM AT3 Biomedical Innovation: a design brochure (PDF on Canvas) plus the practical (a robotic hand
with a camera that plans grasps). School rubric of five criteria, 20 marks each, no bands; the ISEF engineering criteria
were used as extra detail. The task sheet said 14-16 pages; the teacher allowed more.

## What the structure looked like (page titles)
Cover, contents and key events; problem; impact; existing work (four papers); planning (criteria, schedule planned
against actual); materials; risks; first plan; options; development (design cycle); mind map; sketches; final design
(with dimensions); simulation; pipeline; speed; outlines; gripping; grasp plane; steadiness; simulation evaluation;
build process; setting up; camera redesign; apps; testing; grasp test plan; grasp test results; test summary; hand
evaluation; final evaluation; next version; reflection; credits.

## Lessons for the next project
- **Real evidence is where the marks were.** The biggest gaps (creativity, execution and testing) were things only
  the students could supply: own sketches, a hand-drawn mind map, counted real-world trials with photos, decisions in
  their own words. Collect them during the project (skills `build-log`, `test-plan`), not the night before.
- Write the test plan before testing: objects, trials (5+ each), pass mark, conditions. Record every trial.
- Keep a dated log from day one; the schedule (planned against actual) and the development page were built from it.
- A page per rubric section, mapped early (PAGE_PLAN.md), avoids finding a missing section at the end.
- Plan the page count against the limit early; RobotHand grew from 16 to 35 pages and needed the teacher's OK.
- Labelled photos (arrows to the parts) and before/after numbers read better than paragraphs.
- Hand edits in PowerPoint were brought back into the build script before every rebuild, so nothing was lost.
