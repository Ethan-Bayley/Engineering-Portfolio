# Rubric guide: what "strong" looks like

Use this when a school rubric gives criteria but no mark bands. It is guidance, not the school's marking scheme; never
quote it as bands.

## ISEF engineering judging criteria (Society for Science, Regeneron ISEF; read 2026-10-22)
| Section (ISEF points) | What judges look for |
|---|---|
| Research problem (10) | a practical need or problem; criteria for the solution; **the constraints explained** |
| Design and methodology (15) | alternatives explored; a solution identified; a prototype or model developed |
| Execution: construction and testing (20) | the prototype shows the intended design; **tested under multiple conditions or trials**; engineering skill and completeness |
| Creativity and potential impact (20) | significant creativity in one or more of the above; impact on the field, technology, economy, environment or society |
| Presentation: poster (10) | logically organised; clear graphics and legends; supporting documentation shown |
| Presentation: interview (25) | clear answers; understands the science; understands the **limitations**; **degree of independence**; potential impact; quality ideas for **further research** |

Source: https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/

## Common Australian school design-brochure criteria (from the AT3 Biomedical Innovation task, 2026)
| Criterion | Usually asks for |
|---|---|
| Significance | need or problem; engineering goal; existing ideas and alternatives; impact |
| Methodological approaches | criteria and justified materials/technologies; every development stage; risks and a time plan |
| Creativity and innovation | your own sketches, mind maps, CAD, final design, working prototype |
| Execution, construction and testing | engineering skills; testing in multiple conditions/trials; evaluation against criteria (benefits, limits); final evaluation |
| Presentation and communication | colours, fonts, images, white space, annotations; logical order; initial ideas to final design; evidence of independence |

## Gaps that cost the most marks (from past marking loops)
1. No counted tests of the real thing (results "not tested").
2. Sketches or mind maps that are not the student's own.
3. Missing constraints, risks with no likelihood/consequence, no planned-vs-actual time plan.
4. No section headers matching the required sections.
5. Claims with no source or measurement.
